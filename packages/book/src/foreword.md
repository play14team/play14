# Foreword

On March 14th, 2014, 34 people met in Esch/Belval, Luxembourg, to spend a few days playing games. Nobody gave a keynote and nobody sold anything. There was no agenda either: the participants built it themselves every morning.

Twelve years later that small experiment has turned into a community with 93 events in 40 cities and 20 countries, from Luxembourg to Sydney and from Mexico to Mumbai, hosted by volunteers who first came as players and then wanted the same thing in their own city.

This book tells how that happened. It has four parts:

- **How it started** covers the meeting, the name, the logo and the first event in Luxembourg.
- **How we grew** follows the chain of encounters that took #play14 from Luxembourg to 39 other cities. Every city has its own chapter, and the people who brought #play14 there are invited to tell that story in their own words.
- **The mentoring program** explains how we grew without a head office, a marketing budget or a franchise model: experienced hosts help new ones.
- **What makes #play14 special** uses the fourteen words from the talk I gave at GamiCon 38 in March 2026 to describe what you find at a #play14 event.

I'm one of the co-founders, so this is an insider's account, and an incomplete one. Most of the story happened in rooms I wasn't in, with people I met later or have still not met. If you were there and remember it differently, the book is open source: every page has an edit link, and corrections are welcome.

## Why write it down

People always ask us the same questions. How did it start? How does it work without anyone in charge? What makes it different? Over the years I've told the story at dinner tables, in marketplaces and on stage, and every retelling loses a little. This book is my attempt to write it down once, so the next generation of hosts and mentors knows where the community comes from and why it works the way it does.

It's also a book about building a community, and I didn't learn that on my own. I want to thank the other communities I belong to, and two people in particular. Alberto Brandolini was a player at the very first #play14, and he has built a remarkable community around EventStorming. Mike Rohde leads the sketchnoting community. Both showed me that a community grows around a generous idea and the people who share it, not around an organization. Their example runs through these pages.
