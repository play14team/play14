# Learning

<p class="word-tagline">The game is merely an excuse for a debrief.</p>

Learning is the actual goal, and at #play14 it happens by doing.

Participants don't listen to explanations. They try ideas out directly through games and activities. This is experiential learning: you try something, watch what happens, and then reflect on it together.

Through play, complex ideas become much easier to grasp. The main difference is that people find the insights themselves instead of being told what to think. When you've lived something yourself, you remember it better and for longer.

That's why the debrief matters so much. The hosting guide says it plainly: there's nothing sadder than a great game, full of learning opportunities, with no good debrief. A good debrief asks questions instead of explaining what people should have felt or understood. [The Debriefing Cube](https://play14.org/tools/debriefing-cube) is a tool many of us use for that.
