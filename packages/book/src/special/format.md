# Format

<p class="word-tagline">The best sessions are the ones people care about.</p>

#play14 is an unconference based on [Open Space Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_space_technology). There's no fixed program. The participants create the agenda on the spot.

## The marketplace

Every morning, after a few warm-up games, we fill the marketplace: a large board with the available spaces down one side and the day's time slots across the top. Anyone can propose a session, a game or a discussion, pretty much anything, as long as it respects our code of conduct.

Facilitators get about a minute to pitch their game to everyone, so the other players can choose which sessions to join. Then they stick their sticky note in a slot. Time slots and spaces are suggested, but people are free to organize themselves however they like. It's their event.

## The five principles

There are still a few rules.

**Whoever comes is the right people.** If only a few people come to your session, accept it and work with whoever is there. If nobody comes, that's a chance to reflect. Maybe your pitch could have been better, and next time it will be. If too many come, adapt your session, or kindly suggest that some people join another one.

**Whenever it starts is the right time.** If you need five minutes to set up your game, tell the participants and take the time to start properly.

**Wherever it is is the right place.** If you'd rather hold your session somewhere else, or outside because the weather is beautiful, that's fine. Just tell people in advance so they can follow you.

**Whatever happens is the only thing that could have.** If your session goes somewhere you didn't expect, accept it and treat it as a learning opportunity. You may do things differently next time, or discover insights you never expected that turn out to be very useful later.

**When it's over, it's over.** Save time to wrap up your session. Managing the timebox is your job. If people want to keep playing afterwards, that's fine, as long as you free the space for the next game.

## The law of two feet

The key principle is the *law of two feet*, also called the *law of mobility*. If you're not learning or contributing, you're free to move to another session, and nobody takes offense. It keeps the energy high and makes sure everyone is where they want to be.

Some people are *bumblebees*, flying from group to group and cross-pollinating the discussions. Others are *butterflies*, sitting back while interesting conversations form around them. Both are welcome.
