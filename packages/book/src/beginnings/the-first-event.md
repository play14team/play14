# March 14th, 2014

<img class="story-image" src="https://cdn.play14.org/strapi-uploads/assets/technoport_b4526c9464.png" alt="The Technoport building in Esch/Belval">

After only three months of preparation, the first #play14 welcomed **34 players** in Esch/Belval, Luxembourg.

We got the venue for free thanks to Diego De Biasio, then CEO of the [Technoport](http://www.technoport.lu). Diego also helped with the logistics, which mattered a lot for three first-time organizers. We couldn't have done it without him, and that is why we still count Diego as a founding member of #play14, the fourth name next to Yann, Pierre and mine.

## The afternoon nobody planned

From the very first edition, #play14 was an unconference. We had a venue, food and a marketplace, but no program. The participants created the agenda each morning.

My favorite memory from that weekend is the last afternoon. Many people had flights to catch, and only a small group was left. Instead of continuing with the marketplace, we spent the whole afternoon doing improv theater. It was one of the funniest afternoons I've ever had, and it was great precisely because nobody had planned it.

That afternoon taught us something we still believe: the event belongs to the people who are in the room. We come back to that in the chapter called [Open](../special/open.md).

Among the players that weekend was Alberto Brandolini, who has since built a worldwide community around EventStorming. Having someone like him in the room, playing and learning alongside everyone else, set the tone from the start: at #play14 there are no experts on stage. More on that in [Flat](../special/flat.md).
