# A chain of encounters

#play14 never had a growth plan. It grew one person at a time.

In Luxembourg in 2015, I met Chris and Christina, who organized in London afterwards. In London, I met Karsten, who organized in Hamburg. In Hamburg, I met Mari Luz, who organized in Madrid. In Madrid, I met Hanna, who organized in Sydney. In Sydney, I met Fred, who organized in Kuala Lumpur. In Kuala Lumpur, I met Darwin, who organized in the Philippines.

That is one branch. There are many others, and this part of the book follows all of them back to where they started.

## Wider, not bigger

At first we never imagined #play14 would spread to other cities. Our plan was to make the Luxembourg event bigger every year. London changed that. Instead of one big event, #play14 would get *wider*: more cities, each with its own local team, all sharing the same spirit.

That decision shaped everything after it:

- Each event stays small enough that everyone gets to know each other.
- New events start because someone *wants* to host one, not because we went looking for a market.
- Almost every new city starts with someone who played at another city first.
- The people who already know the format help the next team. That later became the [mentoring program](../mentoring/why-mentoring.md).

## How these chapters work

Each city chapter has two halves.

**Where it came from** is my part. It traces the city back to where its first organizers played #play14 and, often, where I met them.

**In their words** belongs to the people who brought #play14 to that city. I've asked each founding team to tell their own story: how they discovered #play14, why they decided to bring it home, and what happened at their first edition. Their chapters will fill in one by one.
